1. The Incretin Effect: How Natural GLP-1 Works

Under normal physiological conditions, when you consume food, your L-cells in the small intestine secrete incretin hormones—primarily GLP-1. This hormone enters the bloodstream and triggers three critical protective responses:

💡 The Three Key Functions of Natural GLP-1

  • Glucose-Dependent Insulin Release: Signals the pancreas to release insulin only when blood sugar rises. Because insulin is produced only in response to elevated glucose, the risk of dangerously low blood sugar (hypoglycemia) is minimized.
  • Glucagon Suppression: Prevents the liver from releasing stored glucose into the bloodstream when it isn't needed.
  • Gastric Delay & Satiety: Slows down the speed at which food leaves the stomach and signals satiety to the brain, helping you feel full faster and stay satisfied longer.

In individuals with Type 2 diabetes, this natural incretin response is often blunted or impaired, leading to post-meal blood sugar spikes and difficulty regulating appetite.

2. What Are GLP-1 Receptor Agonists?

Because natural GLP-1 breaks down in the body within minutes, researchers developed synthetic versions called GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (GLP-1 RAs). In pharmacology, an "agonist" is a molecule that binds to a cell receptor and activates it.

GLP-1 receptor agonists mimic natural GLP-1, but they are modified to resist rapid breakdown. This allows them to circulate continuously—providing long-lasting blood sugar stabilization and metabolic benefits over days or weeks.
